**THRIVE-Belize**

**A life-skills curriculum for Toledo Community College -- and an honest test of whether Toledo wants it**

Presented to the Rotary Club of Punta Gorda by Mario Morales, PhD, University of Arizona

**The full presentation, with every source, stays online at:**

**https://yeridu.github.io/THRIVE-Belize-RotaryClub/**

**What this is**

THRIVE-Belize is a comprehensive life-skills curriculum designed for secondary schools in Toledo District: 30 classroom sessions plus one booster, 40 minutes each, across seven modules covering emotions and communication, mental and physical health, substance use and refusal skills, healthy relationships and assertiveness, sexual and reproductive health, masculinities and boys' health, and environmental health. Alongside the classroom sessions sit whole-school components -- school mapping, an anonymous speak-out box, a School Health Promotion Committee, and school health policies.

**Where it stands right now**

Phase 1 delivers no lessons to anyone. It is a feasibility study that asks roughly 280 people -- 175 students, 75 parents and guardians, 15 teachers, 12 community stakeholders, and 3 school administrators -- whether this curriculum is acceptable, appropriate and workable, and what would have to change before it is ever taught.

The thresholds that would make us stop were written down and published before any data was collected. If the results show scores below 3.0 out of 5, teacher willingness below 40 percent, substantial community opposition, or safeguarding capacity that is not adequate, the study does not proceed. Community opposition is a stopping condition in writing.

**Approvals and registration**

University of Arizona Institutional Review Board, STUDY00007955. Belize Ministry of Health and Wellness Institutional Review Board, MOHW/IRB/2026/002. Belize Ministry of Education, Culture, Science and Technology, GEN/21/26(112) VOL. III. Registered on ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT07687199 on 3 July 2026, before any enrolment.

Students take part only with active written consent from a parent or guardian, followed by their own written assent. Anyone may skip a question or withdraw. Disclosures are handled using the NSPCC 4Rs approach -- Recognise, Respond, Report, Record -- with referral pathways agreed in advance and mandatory reporting under the Belize Families and Children Act.

**Four things we are asking of this club, and none of them is money**

1. Sit with us as community stakeholders. The protocol includes twelve community stakeholders with recognised adolescent-health expertise living in Toledo District. Participation is voluntary, with informed consent, under the approved protocol.

2. Consider chartering an Interact club at Toledo Community College. Interact is Rotary's own programme for young people aged 12 to 18, which is exactly the age range of Forms 1 to 4. It is sponsored by a Rotary club, self-governing, and runs two service projects a year. It is entirely independent of this study and would outlast it.

3. Tell us what we are getting wrong -- on scheduling around the school year and harvest, on language across English, Spanish, Q'eqchi' and Mopan, on reaching parents who will not come to a meeting at the school, and on what has already been tried in Toledo and failed.

4. Later, and only if Phase 1 supports proceeding, we would welcome a conversation about whether a Rotary district or global grant is a fit, given how the modules align with Rotary's areas of focus. Not today.
